About Ramin, a village in Palestine

Ramin is a village in northern Palestine that belongs to the city of Tulkarm. It is a link between the cities of Tulkarm and Nablus and the municipalities of Beit Lid and Bazzariya.

Many thinkers and scholars came from Ramin and helped Palestine develop. It is also one of the biggest producers of figs and olive oil in Palestine, supplying them across the country. In one season, which lasts about two and a half to three months, Ramin yields more than 360 tons of figs.

Landmarks

Ramin has many old landmarks from the Ottoman era. One of them is the Hejaz Railway tunnel, which connected the Levant with the Arabian Peninsula. It also linked the cities of Beisan, Jenin, Tulkarm, Haifa, Jaffa and Acre inside Palestine.

Work began in the late 19th century under Sultan Abdul Hamid II, supervised by the German engineer Heinrich August Meissner. The railway was officially opened in 1912.

Ramin also has a large plain. Residents use its wide area for farming, growing wheat, plums, cherries, figs and olives.

Farming and harvest seasons

Ramin is famous for growing figs and olives. Most families depend on these two seasons to cover their financial needs for the rest of the year.

The fig season runs from April to early August. The olive harvest starts at the beginning of October and lasts until late December.

Ramin Club, First Division champions

Ramin Club, from Ramin village in the Tulkarm governorate of Palestine, won the First Division basketball league title in October.

In the final, Ramin beat Catholic Action at Majed Asaad Hall in the city of Al-Bireh.

Promoted to the Premier League The title takes the team up to the Premier Basketball League after a long absence from that level.
